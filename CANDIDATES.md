# Candidates & Pairwise Tournament

Competition: `u-coral-ibis-18-office-artifacts-the-one-line-placard-3af971d2`
Judge brief (weight order): (1) one-line museum placard for an ordinary office
object as an ancient artifact; (2) inventiveness of the conceit — a specific,
surprising imagined history, not a generic office joke; (3) dryness of wit —
deadpan curator voice, joke landing without straining; (4) economy — every
word earns its place, reads well aloud; (5) label craft — catalogue date,
material, provenance, donor selling the joke.

## The 16 candidates

1. **Stapler** — "Stapler, c. 1997. Pressed steel, red paint. A binding rite: two sheets joined forever by a single bite. Donated by Accounts, who never got it back."
2. **Lanyard** — "Lanyard, c. 2019. Woven polyester, plastic clasp. Worn to prove membership of the tribe; the badge it bore is lost. Gift of Human Resources."
3. **Sticky note** — "Sticky note, c. 2004. Paper, weak adhesive. A prayer left on a monitor, its god having moved desks. Found beneath the fridge."
4. **Microwave** — "Microwave, c. 1989. Steel, humming. A communal hearth in which fish was reheated against all law. Donated by the night shift."
5. **Mouse** — "Mouse, c. 2011. Plastic, optical. A small beast tethered to the desk, its wheel worn smooth by a thousand scrolls. Bequest of IT."
6. **Whiteboard marker** — "Marker, c. 2015. Felt, solvent ink. Used to inscribe prophecies that were wiped clean each Friday. On loan from Facilities."
7. **Desk plant** — "Desk plant, c. 2021. Plastic pot, real soil. Tended by fourteen hands, watered by none, yet it endures. Donor anonymous."
8. **Keyboard** — "Keyboard, c. 2008. ABS plastic, crumbs. Each key a prayer bead, the space bar worn thin by sighs. Excavated from Desk 14."
9. **Paperclip** — "Paperclip, c. 1962. Bent steel wire. One loop that held empires of paper together, then vanished into drawers. Gift of the Stationery Cupboard."
10. **Coffee mug** — "Mug, c. 2016. Ceramic, chipped. 'World's Best Boss' — a title no living witness will confirm. Donated by the leaving party."
11. **Swivel chair** — "Chair, c. 2013. Mesh, five wheels. A throne from which no decision was ever made, only deferred. Recovered from Meeting Room B."
12. **Printer** — "Printer, c. 2005. Plastic, toner. A temperamental oracle; supplicants offered paper and received only blinking lights. On permanent loan."
13. **USB stick** — "Memory stick, c. 2012. Metal, 8 GB. A reliquary said to hold the only copy of the Q3 report. Never opened. Donor unknown."
14. **Fire extinguisher** — "Fire extinguisher, c. 1994. Red steel, untouched. A guardian god, inspected yearly, invoked never. Donated by Health & Safety."
15. **Rubber band** — "Rubber band, c. 2018. Latex, perished. Once stretched around a bundle of invoices; now a relic of optimism. Found in the top drawer."
16. **Hole punch** — "Hole punch, c. 1999. Cast iron, two holes. A ritual instrument for piercing documents so they might be filed — and forgotten. Gift of Archives."

## Tournament bracket (single elimination, judged on the brief in weight order)

### Round 1
- #1 Stapler vs #2 Lanyard → **#2 wins.** "Prove membership of the tribe; the badge is lost" is a more surprising conceit than a binding rite.
- #3 Sticky note vs #4 Microwave → **#3 wins.** "A prayer left on a monitor, its god having moved desks" is more inventive than the (relatable but familiar) fish joke.
- #5 Mouse vs #6 Marker → **#6 wins.** "Prophecies wiped clean each Friday" beats the tethered-beast image on inventiveness and dryness.
- #7 Plant vs #8 Keyboard → **#7 wins.** "Tended by fourteen hands, watered by none, yet it endures" is drier and more paradoxical than the prayer-bead keys.
- #9 Paperclip vs #10 Mug → **#10 wins.** "A title no living witness will confirm" is the sharper deadpan line; the paperclip's empires image is good but looser.
- #11 Chair vs #12 Printer → **#11 wins.** "No decision was ever made, only deferred" is tighter and drier than the oracle printer, which strains slightly.
- #13 USB stick vs #14 Extinguisher → **#14 wins.** "Guardian god, inspected yearly, invoked never" is drier and more surprising than the unopened reliquary.
- #15 Rubber band vs #16 Hole punch → **#16 wins.** "Piercing documents so they might be filed — and forgotten" is the more museum-voiced, deadpan conceit.

### Round 2 (quarter-finals)
- #2 Lanyard vs #3 Sticky note → **#3 wins.** The moved-desks god is the more surprising imagined history; the lost badge is good but one beat shorter on conceit.
- #6 Marker vs #7 Plant → **#7 wins.** The plant's paradox ("tended by fourteen hands, watered by none") lands harder and drier than the Friday prophecies.
- #10 Mug vs #11 Chair → **#11 wins.** The chair's throne-of-deferral is a fresher conceit; the mug leans on the well-worn "World's Best Boss" joke.
- #14 Extinguisher vs #16 Hole punch → **#14 wins.** The guardian-god recast is more surprising than the filing ritual, and "inspected yearly, invoked never" is peak dry curator voice.

### Round 3 (semi-finals)
- #3 Sticky note vs #7 Plant → **#7 wins.** Both inventive; the plant is more deadpan, every word earns its place, and the paradox reads better aloud.
- #11 Chair vs #14 Extinguisher → **#14 wins, by a hair.** Both superb; the extinguisher's guardian-god recast is the more surprising imagined history (criterion 2), and Health & Safety as donor is the stronger label-craft beat (criterion 5).

### Final
- #7 Plant vs #14 Extinguisher → **#14 WINS.** The plant's "tended by fourteen hands, watered by none, yet it endures" is excellent, but the extinguisher wins on criterion 2 (a fire extinguisher as a guardian deity is the more surprising recast of an object nobody looks at twice) and criterion 3/5 ("inspected yearly, invoked never" is the driest line in the field; "Donated by Health & Safety" is the perfect provenance joke). It is also the most economical: 126 characters, no wasted word.
